Five takes on the email

Same content in all five: the address, the AI paragraph about the month, the three changes, the button to the rebuilt report, and the manage line. What varies is which pieces of the site each one borrows. Pick one, or point at the parts you like across two.

A · CardB · Report pageC · TilesD · Question cardsE · Ink
Option A

Card

What's built today. The site's top bar, then one card that holds everything: kicker and the rotated tag, the highlighted address, the AI paragraph, a cream tile with the changes, the button with its sub-label, and the manage line under a hairline.

Why pick it. Reads as one object, like a report card. Compact, and the same shell works for all four emails.

Option B

Report page

The email is the top of the report page: address with the yellow highlight, the borough line, the fact chips, the gist card with its tag, a What changed card with arrow rows, and the full-width button on an ink rule, like the sticky bar.

Why pick it. Most faithful to what they see after clicking, so the handoff feels seamless. Longest of the five.

Option C

Tiles

The home page's What you get grid, repurposed: each change is a tile with the category icon box and the question it belongs to, inside one card. The paragraph sits in the open above it, like the hero.

Why pick it. Scannable and friendly; the icons carry the categories. Needs PNG icons in the real email since Gmail drops SVG.

Option D

Question cards

The report's own question cards: icon box, the question, and a status pill with the colored dot, one card per change. The paragraph lives in a gist card with the tag.

Why pick it. The most informative, and it teaches the report's vocabulary (serious, new case, changed). Busiest.

Option E

Ink

Option A in the site's dark scheme: near-black page, the light-ink borders, coral button, the olive highlight. Matches the site for anyone browsing in dark mode.

Why pick it. Striking in an inbox, and it's exactly what the site looks like at night. Some clients invert dark emails unpredictably, so it needs testing.
